# Test briefs

Five sample projects for testing the Describe step. Each answer is one line, so it's quick to select and paste into its box.

Limits ([config/limits.js](../config/limits.js)): at least 100 characters not counting spaces, at most 500 characters in total. Briefs 1 to 3 are mid-length, brief 4 sits near the minimum and brief 5 near the maximum.

---

## 1. Neighborhood tool library

**Who or what is it?**

A mobile app for apartment buildings and small neighborhoods that turns everyone's garage and closet into a shared tool library: drills, ladders, tents, pressure washers and sewing machines.

**What does it do?**

It lets residents list the items they own, borrow from neighbors within walking distance, book pickup times and leave short condition notes on return. Lenders can ask for a deposit and choose who they trust.

**How does it work?**

Each item gets a QR sticker. Scanning it at pickup and return logs the loan, places and releases the deposit hold, and sends a reminder before the due date. A trust score grows with every on-time return.

**Why does it exist? What problem does it solve?**

Most households own tools they use a few times a year, while the neighbors next door buy the same ones. Sharing saves money, cuts waste and clutter, and gets people who live side by side to actually meet.

## 2. Nurse shift-swap platform

**Who or what is it?**

A scheduling platform for hospital nursing units, used by staff nurses, charge nurses and unit managers to handle open shifts and shift trades without spreadsheets or group chats.

**What does it do?**

Nurses post shifts they can't work, pick up open ones and trade with colleagues. Managers approve swaps in one tap and see staffing levels for every unit, day and night.

**How does it work?**

A rules engine checks each swap against certifications, overtime caps, rest time between shifts and union agreements before it reaches the manager. Approved changes sync to payroll and the hospital roster automatically.

**Why does it exist? What problem does it solve?**

Shift changes still go through text messages and paper, which causes errors, unfair overtime and units left without enough qualified nurses. Exhausted staff quit, and a fair, transparent system helps keep them.

## 3. Ocean plastic sensor buoys

**Who or what is it?**

A network of solar-powered floating sensor buoys run by a coastal research nonprofit, anchored in river mouths and bays where plastic waste enters the sea.

**What does it do?**

It measures how much floating plastic and microplastic passes each point every hour, and publishes the data on a public map that cities, researchers and cleanup crews can use.

**How does it work?**

Cameras and an onboard AI model count debris and sort it by size and type, while a water sampler filters microplastics once a day. Readings are sent by satellite and matched with tide and rainfall data.

**Why does it exist? What problem does it solve?**

Nobody knows exactly where most ocean plastic comes from, so cleanup money is spent blindly. Precise, continuous measurement shows which rivers and storm drains matter most and proves whether plastic bans work.

## 4. Reading buddies for refugee children

**Who or what is it?**

A volunteer reading program for refugee children aged 6 to 12 who are learning English as a new language at their new school.

**What does it do?**

It pairs each child with the same trained volunteer for two short reading sessions a week, online or at the local library.

**How does it work?**

Children read picture books in their home language and in English side by side, with audio, games and a weekly progress note.

**Why does it exist? What problem does it solve?**

Kids who fall behind in reading fall behind in everything. Early support helps them catch up, make friends and feel at home.

## 5. Specialty coffee traceability

**Who or what is it?**

A traceability service for small specialty coffee roasters and the farming cooperatives in Colombia, Ethiopia and Peru that grow their beans. Roasters pay a monthly subscription, cooperatives use it for free, and coffee drinkers meet it through a code printed on every bag they buy, whether in a cafe, a grocery store or an online shop anywhere in Europe. It is run by a small team of former baristas and green coffee buyers who know the trade from both sides of the counter.

**What does it do?**

It follows every coffee lot from the farm to the cup. Drinkers scan the bag to see the farmer, harvest date, altitude, variety, processing method and the price paid to the grower. Roasters get a dashboard of every lot they hold, with cupping scores, stock levels and harvest reports, and can share each lot's story on their website, menus and wholesale price lists without having to write it themselves. Cooperatives see which roasters bought their coffee and how it was rated.

**How does it work?**

Cooperatives record each harvest on a simple phone app that works offline in the mountains and syncs once it finds a mobile signal. Every hand-off along the way (washing station, dry mill, exporter, importer, roaster) adds a signed entry with weights, GPS location and photos, so the full journey of a lot cannot be quietly changed later. Mismatched weights or missing steps are flagged before the coffee is sold as traceable, and the roaster is told what to fix.

**Why does it exist? What problem does it solve?**

Claims like direct trade and fair price are hard to check, and farmers rarely see the value of the quality they work so hard to produce. Verified records build trust with buyers, let roasters reward the farms that grow the best coffee, and give cooperatives proof they can use to negotiate better prices next season. Coffee drinkers finally know where their money goes, and good farming is paid for properly instead of disappearing into an anonymous blend.
